NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Challenge 01

Abandoned but not forgotten.

A digital field archive that helps school-age explorers understand the machines NASA left on the Moon and Mars—and the science those machines made possible.

The problem we address

Space hardware has a second story.

When a lander stops transmitting or a rover can no longer move, its story does not end. The hardware remains on another world, the mission data remains on Earth, and the discoveries still shape exploration.

01Official challenge
Storytelling + science

Our solution

Not a list. A guided investigation.

01

Identify

Explore a curated catalogue of lunar and Martian hardware with filters for destination and mission status.

02

Understand

Each record explains the machine’s mission, what physically stayed behind, what scientists learned, and why it matters.

03

Experience

Story chapters and NASA archive imagery turn technical mission histories into a visual journey for younger audiences.

04

Verify

Every artifact links to primary NASA sources. Active spacecraft are clearly separated from retired hardware.

How the experience works

One machine. Four questions.

01 / PURPOSEWhat was it built to do?
02 / REMAINSWhat stayed on the Moon or Mars?
03 / DISCOVERYWhat did its science teach us?
04 / LEGACYWhy does it still matter?

The archive includes a small number of active or deep-space comparison missions on purpose. They are labelled clearly and are not counted as abandoned surface artifacts.

Global winner benchmark

What we learned from previous winners.

The 2025 global winners show several repeatable strengths. Beyond Relics combines the most relevant patterns for this storytelling challenge rather than copying a single project.

BEST USE OF DATA · 2025

Resonant Exoplanets

Turned large telescope and satellite datasets into an analysis workflow. Our response: make each mission record traceable to original NASA evidence, and make the distinction between hardware status and archived data explicit.

BEST USE OF STORYTELLING · 2025

HerCode Space

Used illustrations and storytelling to explain space weather to younger audiences. Our response: story chapters, plain-language science, and four consistent questions for every artifact.

ART & TECHNOLOGY · 2025 · EGYPT

Zumorroda-X

Used mini-games to make NASA Earth data accessible through play. Our response: a visual, navigable archive with filters and a clear exploration path—without distracting from the mission facts.

BEST USE OF SCIENCE · 2025

SpaceGenes+

Connected scientific evidence to an interactive research dashboard. Our response: source-linked mission records, measurable facts, and a visible evidence trail for each story.

BEST USE OF SCIENCE · 2023 · EGYPT

LunarTech Ensemble

Built an immersive game around lunar seismic measurements from Apollo instruments. This is a close benchmark: Beyond Relics must let visitors investigate mission evidence, not only read mission summaries.

BEST STORYTELLING · 2023

TeamVoyagers

Used an interactive web game to explain the water cycle. The lesson is to make visitors discover the science through the experience, not just move between static captions.

BEST STORYTELLING · 2024 · EGYPT

TerraTales

Combined climate stories, an AI forecasting model, and an interactive game. It demonstrates how a clear narrative can be strengthened by a working, evidence-based interaction.

Global judging · Data eligibility

A source link is not the same as using a dataset.

The NASA Space Apps Global Organizing Team clarified that Global Award eligibility requires meaningful use of at least one NASA data source listed in the Resources tab of this exact challenge. Citing mission facts or showing NASA photographs alone does not satisfy that requirement.

What Beyond Relics currently demonstrates

NASA PDS archive metadata, published ALSEP station coordinates and instrument sampling specifications, plus NASA Image and Video Library records.

What must be confirmed before submission

Check the official Challenge 01 Resources tab and record which listed dataset is used. The Apollo PSE archive is a NASA PDS dataset, but its presence in that challenge-specific resource list has not been independently confirmed here.

What makes the data use convincing

Show the exact source, the fields or observations extracted, the transformation or comparison performed, and the resulting finding in the visitor experience.

Judging alignment

Five criteria. Visible proof.

Impact

Make the science and engineering legacy of real space hardware understandable to school-age learners.

Proof: learning-first artifact stories and a guided archive.
Creativity

Connect physical remains, mission status, science outcomes, and storytelling in one experience.

Proof: four-question artifact format and visual story reader.
Validity

Use verified mission facts and link each record to primary NASA sources.

Proof: source lists, mission dates, status labels, and transparent scope.
Relevance

Focus the main collection on equipment left on the Moon and Mars.

Proof: lunar and Martian artifact records; active spacecraft clearly marked as comparisons.
Presentation

Give judges a direct route from the challenge problem to the interactive experience.

Proof: this brief, responsive archive, story chapters, and a clear demo path.

The 2026 event’s published evaluation criteria include Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance, and Presentation. Always follow the final instructions in the official team submission portal.

The core evidence journey

From a machine left on the Moon to a signal we can still study.

01 / THE HARDWAREApollo 12 deployed a passive seismic station on the lunar surface.
02 / THE OBSERVATIONThe station recorded ground motion; its measurements became part of a preserved science archive.
03 / THE DATABeyond Relics reads an original Apollo PSE GeoCSV file and plots its numeric observations.
04 / THE LEGACYThe instrument is no longer operating, but its archived evidence remains available for investigation.

The waveform view is a transparent plot with descriptive statistics. It does not identify a moonquake, impact, or cause. The exact Challenge 01 Resources-list match for this dataset must still be confirmed before claiming Global Award data eligibility.

Judge-ready walkthrough · 30 seconds

Lead with the evidence. End with the human story.

  1. 0–5 sec: State the challenge: machines can fall silent while their physical remains and scientific legacy endure.
  2. 5–12 sec: Open the Apollo 12 artifact and identify the passive seismic instrument left on the Moon.
  3. 12–22 sec: Open the NASA Data Lab, load the Apollo 12 GeoCSV observation, and point to the plotted values and calculated statistics.
  4. 22–30 sec: Explain the connection: a retired instrument still leaves evidence that people can study today. Finish on the story page.

AI and source transparency

How digital tools were used.

ChatGPT assisted with code development and editorial refinement. The project team remains responsible for reviewing the implementation and checking mission claims against the linked primary sources. Mission imagery is retrieved from NASA’s public Image and Video Library; no AI-generated mission images are presented as NASA photographs.

Beyond Relics is an independent educational project. NASA names and imagery are credited to their original records and are not an endorsement of this project.

Research integrity

What this project does—and does not claim.

Beyond Relics is an independent educational project, not an official NASA product. It does not claim to track spacecraft in real time, reconstruct raw telemetry, or provide a complete inventory of every object left in space. It uses selected, source-linked mission records and NASA’s public image library. If a mission is still active, the interface says so.
